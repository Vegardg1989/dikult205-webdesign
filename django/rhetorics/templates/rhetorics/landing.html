The Rhetorics: one news story, two persuasive websites

The task in DIKULT205 was to take one real news story and build two websites ("worlds") that use the same facts to persuade two opposing audiences, then write a rhetorical analysis of both.

The story: in January 2020 the Chicago Tribune reported that a one-year-old was badly wounded while his parents fought over a gun. The father first told the hospital that a gunman had fired at him and hit the boy.

World 1: The Crime Lab Project

A research lab that argues with data: youth jobs and precision policing reduce gun violence. Logos and ethos: statistics, charts and the credibility of research.

World 2: Chicago Rifle Association

A gun owners' association that argues from the Second Amendment. Pathos: the eagle, the stars and stripes, and freedom. Members log in and rate ranges, and admins write the news.

How each world uses the facts

The boy survived in serious but stable condition.Links the case to the lab's own founding story: acting is better than ignoring the problem.Accidents happen every day. The answer is education, safe storage and mental health support.
The father lied about a gunman.False reports waste police resources that targeted policing depends on.Being a law-abiding citizen is what protects the right to keep and bear arms.

What changed in the rewrite

The 2020 version was PHP with MySQL on the university's server, which no longer exists. This version is Python and Django and keeps the original layouts and stylesheets. Two things were left out on purpose: the real organisations' logos (World 1 originally used the University of Chicago Crime Lab logo, and World 2 showed the NRA as a "partner"), so the live demo can't be mistaken for those organisations.
