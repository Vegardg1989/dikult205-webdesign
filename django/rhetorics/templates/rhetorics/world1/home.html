Working with the community for over a decade.

In over a decade, the crime lab has worked closely with civic and community leaders to design and create programs to reduce crime and violence in the Chicago area. In the year of 2007, a student who studied at our University was tragically murdered as part of a robbery. That tragic incident led to a process of soul-searching at the University, where we realized the lack of influence we were having in the surrounding communities. We asked ourselves, what can we do to help?

A couple of years before we started our project, the National Academies of Science released a report, where our summary of it revealed that the scientific enterprise had learned little about the process of effectively reducing gun violence in the USA. This problem was and is something we want to address. When we started the project over a decade ago, we decided to work closely with the government, non-profit organizations, and philanthropists to create innovative solutions to the problems our city faces through the usage of big data.

Gun violence affects whole communities across the city, but a relatively small group of people are disproportionately involved. These are often men who have experienced traumatic events and have disconnected from school and have tenuous job opportunities. They are very significant in the process of reducing crime and creating a peaceful atmosphere in the city.
