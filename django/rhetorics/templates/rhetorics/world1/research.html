A public health perspective on gun violence.

Gun violence is a serious public health problem. Thousands are killed and harmed each year, children among them, as a recent story in the Chicago Tribune shows: a toddler was severely wounded as his parents struggled over a gun.

Jobs change outcomes.

In a randomised study of a Chicago summer jobs programme, teenagers who were offered a job were 43% less likely to be arrested for a violent crime than those who were not. Data like this is why we argue that jobs for young people are violence prevention.

Heat and shootings.

Shootings in Chicago peak on hot summer days. Why is still heatedly debated: some research suggests heat makes people more irritable and quicker to anger. We report the pattern and do not claim more than the data shows.
